Other free hosts, and the guardrails on every free plan

GitHub Pages is not the only free place to put a static site. Cloudflare, Netlify, and Vercel all do the same job with different extras — and every free plan comes with limits that can pause your site. Learn to read them before you depend on them.

Why look beyond Pages

Lesson 6.3 showed that Pages turns a repository into a website for free, and for a plain static site that is usually enough. Your mission is to ship a link that works for anyone, and sometimes a different host fits better: a nicer address, automatic preview links for every branch, or a build step that Pages handles differently. The good news is that the core idea never changes. A host is a service that stores your files and answers browsers that ask for them; switching hosts changes the domain in your address, not the shape of your folder.

What does change is the small print. Every free plan has usage limits — caps on builds, files, traffic, or deploys — and when you hit one, the host may slow down or stop your site until the next cycle. Reading that small print is the skill of this lesson.

Three popular alternatives

Cloudflare Pages, Netlify, and Vercel all work the same way at a beginner's level: you create a free account, connect a git repository (or drop in a folder of files), and each push becomes a new deploy — a published version of your site. Each gives you a free address on their own domain, such as my-app.pages.dev, my-app.netlify.app, or my-app.vercel.app. Signing up and signing in happen in your browser and are always your steps; no agent can log in for you.

How to read a guardrail

Cloudflare's limits page states it plainly: Cloudflare Pages sites can contain up to 20,000 files on the Free plan, and the maximum file size for a single Cloudflare Pages site asset is 25 MiB.[1] Its build table lists 500 builds per month on Free and warns that builds will timeout after 20 minutes.[1] If your project has 30,000 files, Cloudflare's free plan is the wrong tool — no setting fixes that.

Netlify now bills new accounts in credits: the Free plan includes 300 credits/month with a hard limit and no auto recharge option.[2] Traffic is metered — web bandwidth costs 20 credits per GB — and production deploys consume 15 credits.[2] So a busy month can run the tank dry and stop your deploys, which is exactly what a hard limit means: nothing over the line until next month.

Vercel's Hobby plan is also free but adds a policy guardrail, not only a number: the Hobby plan restricts users to non-commercial, personal use only.[4] Its table gives First 100 GB of fast data transfer and 100 deployments per day, and it warns that if you exceed limits you will have to wait until 30 days have passed.[3] Read guardrails as two kinds: quotas (numbers you can measure, like files or GB) and policies (rules about what the plan is for).

A real pattern, anonymised

A user had a small site live on GitHub Pages and wanted the same files on a second host as a backup. They asked the agent to “set it up on another free host.” The agent prepared the deploy steps but stopped at sign-in: only a human can log in, in a browser. The user ran the login, checked the account page, and confirmed the account was still on the free plan before telling the agent to deploy. The deploy then worked, and the backup site was live.

Two habits to copy: logins stay with you, and “free plan” is something you verify on the account page — the agent cannot see it.

Recall before you read on

Without scrolling up: name one numeric limit on Cloudflare Pages' free plan, and one rule on Vercel's Hobby plan that is a policy rather than a number. Then say which of the three hosts you would check first for a site with 40,000 small files.

Cloudflare's free plan caps a site at 20,000 files (also 25 MiB per file, 500 builds per month). Vercel's Hobby plan is restricted to non-commercial personal use. A site with 40,000 files exceeds Cloudflare's free file cap, so check Vercel or Netlify first — and re-read their current limits, because plans change.

What changes and what does not

Switching hosts changes the domain in front of your files; it does not change your files. The relative-link habit from lesson 6.3 is exactly why: links between your own pages are written without any domain, so they survive a move from github.io to pages.dev, netlify.app, or vercel.app unchanged. What you must re-check after moving is the new host's guardrails against your real numbers — file count, biggest file, expected visitors — and its deploy settings, which you confirm in the host's dashboard. Adding your own domain name on top is lesson 6.10; rollback if a deploy goes wrong is lesson 6.9.

Check yourself

Question 1 of 4

A free hosting plan is best described as…

Every free plan publishes usage limits, and hosts enforce them — by pausing features or stopping deploys until the next cycle. The other options describe a plan with no limits, which does not exist.

Question 2 of 4

On Cloudflare Pages' free plan, one site may contain up to…

Cloudflare's limits page states up to 20,000 files per site on the Free plan and 25 MiB per single asset. Five hundred is the builds-per-month figure, not the file count.

Question 3 of 4

Vercel's free Hobby plan is restricted to…

Vercel's fair-use guidelines restrict the Hobby plan to non-commercial, personal use; commercial projects need a paid plan. The 100 GB figure is a monthly data-transfer quota, not a minimum site size.

Question 4 of 4

A new Netlify account on the free plan receives…

Netlify's free plan includes 300 credits per month with a hard limit and no auto-recharge; bandwidth and production deploys each consume credits. The thousand-credit figure belongs to the paid Personal plan.

Read this next (primary sources)

Cloudflare Pages limits — the Free-plan column is the one to memorise. Then Netlify's credit-based plans for how credits are spent, and Vercel's Hobby plan plus its fair-use guidelines.

Ask your teacher

Anything unclear? Ask the agent that gave you this course. Try: “Compare my publish folder against the free plans of Cloudflare, Netlify, and Vercel, and tell me which limit I would hit first on each” or “My site has 40,000 files — which free host fits?” Questions are the point.
